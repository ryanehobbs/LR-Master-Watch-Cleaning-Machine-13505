## U.S. Patents listed on the plate
Three patents are cast/stamped on the nameplate — all **"Watch Cleaning Machine"** patents by **Frank Regero**, the machine's designer (assigned to L&R Mfg. Co.). **All three are now verified from the granted patent documents** (copies in `reference/patents/`). ✅ **Complete set.**

### What each patent covers (from the granted drawings)
- **1,817,266 (1931) — the foundational machine.** Fig. 1 shows the whole architecture we restored: a vertical support post, a swiveling operating arm/handle (to index the head over each station), the motor mounted up top, and a **mesh basket that lowers into the jars**. Fig. 5 details the two-piece screen basket with a clamped lid; Fig. 4 shows the motor end with its cooling fan. This is the core watch-cleaning-machine concept.
- **1,872,812 (1932) — speed control + star basket.** Fig. 1 shows a toothed **speed-control disk** (ref. 29/30) mounted below the motor on the post, and Fig. 2 shows the **six-point star basket** profile seated in a jar — the mechanical DNA of this machine (the rheostat-driven variable speed and the fluted basket). This is the number molded into the specimen's gasket.
- **1,907,366 (1933) — the spherical motor housing + refinements.** Fig. 1 shows the distinctive **round/ball motor housing** (matching our upper housing UH-001) on the swivel arm, with the basket lowering into a jar; Fig. 1a shows a star/hex basket profile. This is the later improvement patent that gives the Master its familiar spherical motor dome.

> **Historical note.** Both verified patents name **Frank Regero** as inventor — the designer behind the L&R watch-cleaning machine line. The progression 1,817,266 (filed 1929) → 1,907,366 (filed 1930) tracks the evolution from the general machine to the spherical-motor "Master" form factor this specimen represents.

> **Plate set complete.** All three nameplate patents are verified and their granted-document PDFs are filed in `reference/patents/`. No further patents apply to specimen 13505.
