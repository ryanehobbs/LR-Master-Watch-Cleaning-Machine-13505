## R-1 — Gasket / jar seal reconditioning
- **Part ID:** **PLT-002** (tapered jar seal gasket) — the single molded rubber piece carrying the molded mark **U.S. PATENT 1,872,812**. Tapered to wedge into the glass jar mouth and seal the jar around the spinning basket so cleaning solution doesn't splash out. *(Confirmed = the conditioned gasket, not a separate cap seal.)* **Note:** patent 1,872,812 is L&R's 1930/1932 "Watch Cleaning Machine" patent (inventor Frank Regero) covering the basket-mounting mechanism — molded here as a **maker's mark, not a seal patent** (CONFIRMED via Google Patents 2026-06-20; see CLAUDE.md External Reference Intel).
- **As-found condition:** rubber was **dried out and almost degraded** — stiff, brittle, losing pliability; risked cracking/failure if used as-is. Surface carried an orange-brown chemical film.
- **Materials/products:** **SEKODAY Treadmill Belt Lubricant** — components: **silicone oil (non-cracking type), 100%** (Jiashan Sekoday Trading Co.; bought on Amazon). Sold as a treadmill-belt lubricant but it is pure silicone oil — appropriate for the rubber gasket. Warm Citranox solution for the pre-clean. *(Product name CONFIRMED by box photo 2026-06-20.)*
- **Tools:** soak vessel; microfiber cloth.
- **Technique / step sequence:**
  1. Cleaned with warm Citranox solution to remove the orange-brown chemical film (patent text came back crisp).
  2. **Immersion soak** in the SEKODAY silicone (16+ hours) to fully re-saturate the rubber.
  3. Microfiber-buffed to a full satin-black finish.
- **Result:** rubber **fully re-saturated and pliability restored** — structurally sound again, patent text crisp.
- **Caution:** **WD-40 ruled out** for rubber contact — petroleum distillates swell and degrade vintage elastomers. Use silicone only. (The SEKODAY box confirms it: silicone has "a good effect on plastic/rubber products and cannot replace metal lubricants" — so silicone for the gasket, oil for the bearings.)
- **Photo:** see `photos/gasket/`.
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) — done and verified (pliability restored); product name CONFIRMED 2026-06-20 (SEKODAY Treadmill Belt Lubricant = silicone oil, non-cracking type).

## R-2 — Cosmetic refinishing of the motor housings (strip → prep → wrinkle topcoat → cure)
- **Part IDs:** **UH-001** (upper housing) + **LH-001** (lower housing), both halves; mating flanges **FLG-001**; cooling vents **UH-007**; internal bores/chambers masked off.
- **As-found condition:** decades of factory **baked-on enamel, oil varnish, and grease** — a cross-linked finish bonded into the porous aluminum castings.
- **Materials/products:** **Citristrip** (citrus-based paint & varnish stripper gel — safe on aluminum); **99.9% IPA** for the final pre-paint wipe; **Rust-Oleum 249322 Self-Etching Primer** (zinc-phosphate base); **VHT SP201 Wrinkle Plus** high-temp topcoat (black); **high-temperature automotive masking tape**.
- **Tools:** submersion/soak vessel; wire brushes + detailing picks; abrasive pad / wire wheel; razor blade (mask trimming); spray cans; heat gun (and/or oven).
- **Technique / step sequence:**
  - **Phase 1 — Chemical strip & degrease:** coated the castings with **Citristrip stripper gel** and let it dwell to lift the cross-linked factory finish (citrus-based, **safe on the aluminum substrate**); used **wire brushes + detailing picks** to work the softened finish out of the deep cooling fins, internal chambers, and anti-rotation notches; **removed all stripper residue (scrape / wipe / rinse) and dried immediately** to prevent flash-rust / white oxidation on the raw aluminum.
  - **Phase 2 — Surface prep & masking:** lightly **scuffed the exterior raw aluminum** with an abrasive pad / wire wheel to give the primer "tooth"; **precision-masked the running faces** with razor-trimmed high-temp tape — the **0.800" total-height internal chamber**, the **0.600" upper bore** + **0.500" bottom exit bottleneck shelf**, the **0.400" inner clearance channel walls**, and **all mating flanges (FLG-001)** where the end-bells bolt together.
  - **Phase 2.5 — Final IPA wipe + self-etching primer:** **immediately before priming, wiped everything down with 99.9% IPA** (skin oils / stray turbine oil / chemical film), then applied **Rust-Oleum 249322** in ultra-thin continuous coats; matte olive-drab/grey appearance confirmed a proper anchor.
  - **Phase 3 — VHT Wrinkle Plus topcoat (heavy 3-coat crosshatch):** wrinkle finish needs a thick, heavy film to crowd the molecules so they ripple — laid down 3 heavy crisscrossing coats: **Coat 1 horizontal → Coat 2 vertical (5-min flash) → Coat 3 diagonal (5-min flash)** for uniform multidirectional thickness over the rounded contours; kept **flash windows tight** so the layer stayed one singular bound wet film (no premature skinning).
  - **Phase 4 — Thermal cure / wrinkle activation:** applied **controlled heat (heat gun and/or oven, ~350°F after the level window)** to draw out a uniform texture across the varying metal thickness; the thick wet coat shifted from gloss liquid to a tight, uniform industrial matte wrinkle. **Masking pulled while the film was still warm** to preserve internal mechanical dimensions.
- **Result:** tight, uniform heavy wrinkle grain on both housing halves, matching the vintage L&R Master aesthetic; mechanical tolerances preserved. Cure complete — ready for reassembly.
- **Cautions:** ⚠️ remove all Citristrip residue and **dry immediately** (raw aluminum flash-rusts fast); ⚠️ wrinkle texture only develops with a **heavy film + tight flash times** and **uniform heat**; ⚠️ mask every running face/bore/flange; ⚠️ pull masking **while warm**.
- **Photo:** see `photos/housing/`.
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) done & verified. NOTE: the finish was stripped with **Citristrip** (paint/varnish stripper gel) — earlier drafts wrongly named "Citranox"; **Citranox was used only on some mesh basket pieces** (see R-3), not the housings. Coat order is **horizontal → vertical → diagonal** (an earlier summary listed vertical/horizontal/uniform). CLAUDE.md reconciled to match.

## R-3 — General ultrasonic cleaning / degrease of small parts (during disassembly)
- **Part IDs:** essentially **all small disassembled hardware that fit the tank** — bearings (BRG-001/002), fasteners (FLG-002, STR-008, UH-004/005, SPN-005/007, PLT-005), shaft collars (ARM-008/009), washers, oil feed tube (LH-006), small brackets, etc. **Oversized parts that did not fit the tank** (housings, base/body casting, neck/pillar) were **cleaned by hand**.
- **As-found condition:** service grime — old turbine/cleaning-fluid oil, grease, and light surface oxidation on the disassembled parts.
- **Materials/products:** **mild soap (dish/parts detergent), diluted**, in warm water, run in the **ultrasonic tank**. Mild/neutral chosen deliberately — safe across the mixed materials (steel, brass, aluminum, phenolic). (Finish stripping used Citristrip — R-2; Citranox was reserved for some mesh basket pieces.)
- **Tools:** ultrasonic cleaning tank; hand brushes / cloths for oversized parts.
- **Technique / step sequence:** as each sub-assembly came apart during teardown, small parts were dropped into the ultrasonic tank with the **diluted mild-soap solution** to degrease and clean, then rinsed and dried. Anything too large for the tank was cleaned by hand.
- **Result:** all hardware degreased and clean — ready for inspection, measurement, and reassembly.
- **Cautions:** ⚠️ **ultrasonic degreasing strips all lubrication** — re-oil the bearings (BRG-001/002) and wear surfaces before/at reassembly (see R-7/R-8); ⚠️ **dry parts promptly** (raw steel/aluminum flash-rusts); ⚠️ keep the chemistry mild — aggressive cleaners can attack aluminum/phenolic.
- **Related (heavier clean):** **some of the mesh basket pieces (BSK-002)** carried baked-on chemical-fluid sludge and got a dedicated **Citranox** clean to cut it — a stronger detergent than the general mild-soap bath (CONFIRMED 2026-06-20).
- **Photo:** see `photos/` (per-assembly subfolders: `bearings/`, `hardware/`, `basket/`).
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) general mild-soap ultrasonic clean during teardown; some mesh basket pieces cleaned with Citranox (CONFIRMED).

## R-4 — Bearing system rebuild: materials selection & sourcing
*Companion to the install procedures **R-7** (lower housing) and **R-8** (upper housing) — this entry covers the overhaul strategy, what was worn vs sound, and the material choices/sourcing. The step-by-step installs live in R-7/R-8.*
- **Part IDs:** spherical sleeve bearings **BRG-001** (upper) / **BRG-002** (lower); reservoir yarn **BRG-008**; felt washers **BRG-009** (large) / **BRG-010** (small); lower retaining ring **BRG-011**; conical tension springs **BRG-012**; oil wick (GE); oil.
- **As-found condition:** the **bearings themselves were sound** (self-aligning spherical sleeve type — cleanable and reusable). The **consumables were spent:** original felt washers were **brittle and broke apart** on disassembly; the reservoir was a **natural wool-felt yarn/string** threaded through the bearing's central lube hole/slit and wrapped around the globe — dried out, oil long gone. *(as-found = RECALL from teardown + R-7 dictation.)*
- **Reused (original, not replaced):**
  - **BRG-001 / BRG-002 bearings** — cleaned (general mild-soap ultrasonic, R-3) and reused; geometry confirmed good (see bearings dimension sheet).
  - **BRG-012 conical springs** — **original factory springs reused** (CONFIRMED). The **Century Spring TA-2226CS** was identified as the dimensional equivalent / source-if-needed (302/304 SS, large OD 0.750", small OD 0.500", free length 0.440", wire 0.0625") but was **not installed**.
- **Replaced consumables (new stock + rationale):**
  - **Reservoir yarn (BRG-008):** **Estako Wool 98 — 100% Superwash Merino Worsted, off-white/un-dyed.** Chosen un-dyed so it **won't leach dye or mat** when oil-saturated; merino gives good capillary draw to feed oil to the shaft.
  - **Felt washers (BRG-009 / BRG-010):** **SAE F3 hard wool felt** — replaces the disintegrated originals; hard felt holds shape as a dust block + oil-containment washer at the pocket edges.
  - **Oil wick:** **GE 1/8" vintage-fan oil wick** (~1¼" cut), tied to the yarn to carry oil through the port / bearing slit.
  - **Oil:** **Bramec Zoom Spout** multi-purpose turbine oil.
- **Original factory lube design (reference, drives the material choices):** a continuous natural yarn threaded through the central lube hole to contact the shaft, the remainder wrapped around the bearing globe; flat wool-felt washers at the pocket edges for dust blocking and oil containment. The modern stock above mirrors that scheme.
- **Result:** a complete, matched consumable set ready to install — executed in **R-7** (lower) and **R-8** (upper).
- **Cautions:** ⚠️ ultrasonically cleaned bearings are **bone-dry — must be re-oiled** before/at install; ⚠️ use **un-dyed** yarn (dye can leach into cleaning solvents); ⚠️ keep felt + yarn **oil-saturated** for the capillary feed to work.
- **Photo:** see `photos/bearings/`.
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) materials + reuse decisions (springs + bearings reused); as-found condition (RECALL) from teardown/R-7 dictation.

## R-6 — Platform gasket re-fastening (rivet → serviceable screw set)
- **Part IDs:** PLT-005 (gasket deck fastener), PLT-001 (platform disk), PLT-002 (tapered jar seal gasket).
- **As-found:** the tapered rubber gasket (PLT-002) was **riveted** to the metal platform disk (PLT-001) by **4 permanent rivets** through 4 of the 6 holes on the 2.300" circle. The rivets had to be **drilled out** to remove and service the gasket — they are not reusable.
- **Restoration decision (terminal fix):** replace the 4 rivets with a **serviceable screw-and-nut set** so the gasket can be removed for future service — consistent with the main motor-cap gasket hardware upgrade (screw-and-nut for even compression / serviceability).
- **Hardware:** per hole (×4) — **stainless M4-0.7 × 10 mm flat-head Phillips screw + stainless M4-0.7 hex nut**, reusing the **original flat washers (PLT-006, OD 0.4385")**. Only the rivet shank was replaced; the washers are original and retained.
- **Technique / notes:** the **flat (countersunk) Phillips head** gives the flush finish — no trimming needed (resolved the earlier length question). New screw + nut are **stainless steel**. Original rivet dimensions are unrecoverable (destroyed on removal).
- **Result:** gasket now serviceable/removable; deck fastening restored.
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) decision + hardware; final screw length (VERIFY after trimming).
- **Note for drawings:** the engineering sheets are reverse-engineered ORIGINAL design — show the **original rivet** as the design of record, with a note that the restoration substituted the M4 screw/nut/washer (this entry is the citation).

## R-7 — Lower-housing bearing reinstallation (Reassembly Phase 1)
*Source: voice dictation 2026-06-19 (5 memos). Raw: `reassembly_bearings_dictation_raw.txt`. Note: no public instructions exist for this — method is the restorer's reconstruction from the as-found wick/felt arrangement.*
- **Part IDs / materials:** lower housing **LH-001**; lower bearing **BRG-002** (notched at the bottom — installs one way only, must seat in the notch); conical tension spring **BRG-012**; large + small **SAE F3 felt washers** (**BRG-009** large / **BRG-010** small); bottom **metal retaining ring BRG-011**; oil-wick reservoir = **wool yarn (BRG-008)** tied to a **GE 1/8" oil wick** (the type for 1920s–30s fans), cut ≈ 1¼"; oil = **Bramec Zoom Spout** multi-purpose turbine oil.
- **Procedure (as performed):**
  1. Cut ≈ 1 ft of wool yarn and ≈ 1¼" of the 1/8" oil wick; **tie the yarn to the wick** with tight knots.
  2. **Route the yarn through the oil-wick port.** The yarn is floppy — stiffen the lead end with a small wrap of **Scotch tape**, then pull it through with **tweezers**.
  3. **Small felt washer (BRG-010)** sits **flush at the bottom of the bearing** (it contacts the incoming wick → that's how the lower felt gets oiled). Route the yarn **up the side** of the bearing; wrap it in concentric turns around the **top neck**, capturing the **large felt washer (BRG-009)** on top (oil wicks up the yarn to the upper felt — capillary feed).
  4. Seat the **bearing (BRG-002)** into the housing — mind the **bottom notch** (one orientation only).
  5. **Pre-lubricate** the felts + yarn with Zoom Spout oil; let it sit on plastic to soak (capillary).
  6. Insert the **tension spring (BRG-012)** into the housing.
  7. Install the **retaining ring (BRG-011)** at the very bottom over the felt — **friction fit**; tap it down with a **brass flat punch (Grace USA)** + light **rubber-mallet** taps, working **evenly around the ring** until flush.
  8. **Clean the bearing bore** with alcohol prep pads (clear install debris).
  9. **Test-fit the armature** — fit confirmed good.
- **Cautions:** ⚠️ **No steel tools under pressure** — steel mars the bearing/housing. Use **brass punches** (soft, won't mar) and light, even force only.
- **New/confirmed dimension — BRG-011 retaining ring:** OD **0.500"**, ID **0.295"**, thickness **1/16" (0.0625")** (CONFIRMED). (This is the existing BRG-011, now measured + characterized as a friction-fit ring; flag for a drawing callout.)
- **Note (bearing generations):** this wick/felt capillary oiling is the **early OILED sleeve-bearing** design (our machine). Later Masters used **sealed** bearings (manual: "never requires oiling"); a roller-bearing variant is hobbyist-claimed but unconfirmed. (The "Mark 1/Mark 2" labels are collector shorthand, not L&R nomenclature — see CLAUDE.md External Reference Intel.) Effectiveness of the capillary feed vs. a sealed setup is unknown, but it's the original design intent for this generation.

## R-8 — Upper-housing bearing reinstallation (Reassembly Phase 1, cont.)
*Source: voice dictation 2026-06-20 (4 memos). Raw: `reassembly_upper_dictation_raw.txt`. No public instructions exist — method is the restorer's reconstruction from the as-found wick/felt arrangement. This was the harder of the two installs (15+ failed attempts before a working method).*
- **Part IDs / materials:** upper housing **UH-001**; upper bearing **BRG-001** (has an internal top-to-bottom **slit/channel** in the bore); conical tension spring **BRG-012**; **large felt washer BRG-009** (center cutout, same as lower); **upper small felt = a solid felt TAB/PAD** (NOT a cutout washer — sits at the very top/outer, under the oil cap; has only a tiny pin-hole); reservoir = **wool felt yarn (BRG-008)**; oil access cap **UH-011** ("OIL"-stamped silver dome); oil = **Bramec Zoom Spout**. **No BRG-011 retaining ring** (upper housing does not use one).
- **Key differences vs R-7 (lower housing):**
  1. **Yarn rides INSIDE the bearing** — routed through the bearing's internal slit/channel (the original showed yarn wrapped through this slit and over the top), versus the lower housing where the yarn rides the OUTSIDE of the ball.
  2. **Top felt is a tab/pad, not a washer** — no center cutout, just a pin-hole; it sits at the very top under the **oil cap (UH-011)**. Oiling sequence: oil added at the top → wets the felt pad → capillary-wicks down the slotted yarn → lubricates the bottom washer + shaft.
  3. **No bottom retaining ring** (no BRG-011) — bearing + large washer + spring only.
- **Procedure (as performed — the working method, after many failures):**
  1. Cut wool felt yarn; goal is to mirror the original slit-routing.
  2. **Failed approaches (recorded for the manual):** knotting both ends to the channel width let the felt detach/loosen the moment the bearing was seated — the yarn is too pliable (not a stiff rope) to hold knots under handling.
  3. **Working method (~15th attempt):** tie the yarn around a small **round wood peg (~0.400" dia)** with a clove-hitch/hitch knot, add a second knot to secure → slip that knot over the **bearing end nearest the housing interior**; route the other end **through the bearing's internal channel**; wrap it **tightly around the top** of the bearing (the end nearest the oil cap / small felt); **dab a little Loctite Super Glue** on the terminal end to lock it (needed one anchored end).
  4. On the lead end closest to the small felt pad, stiffen with a touch of **Scotch tape** and thread it up through a small poked hole in the felt; trim.
  5. **Seat the bearing (BRG-001)** — once glued it seated properly with no detachment.
  6. Fit the **large felt washer (BRG-009)** on top.
  7. **Pre-lubricate** everything with Zoom Spout oil.
  8. Insert the **tension spring (BRG-012)**.
  9. **Test-fit the armature** — seats and **spins fine**, shaft lubed.
  10. **Cap the oil port** — silver **OIL cap (UH-011)** back on the top of the housing; the small felt pad sits just underneath it.
- **Cautions / open question:** ⚠️ same no-steel-under-pressure rule as R-7. ⚠️ **Effect of the super-glue anchor on long-term oil wicking is unknown** (restorer's note) — flag **(VERIFY)**: watch oil flow/feed over time; the glue was a last resort to keep the pliable felt from detaching during seating, with no manual to reference.

## R-9 — Armature into lower housing + ARM-010 slinger seated (Reassembly Phase 2 — start)
*As-performed 2026-06-21 (Father's Day). Source: voice dictation, 3 memos → `reassembly_r9_dictation_raw.txt`. First mechanical-assembly step: marries the armature to the lower housing and presses the external slinger onto the lower journal.*
- **Part IDs:** armature **ARM-001** (lower journal **SHF-003**); lower housing **LH-001** (with **BRG-002** + felt/spring already installed, R-7); **4 phenolic end-float washers** — **ARM-012** (small) + **ARM-011 ×3** (large); **ARM-010** brass slinger.
- **Procedure (as performed):**
  1. **Stack the 4 phenolic washers on the fan/lower-journal end of the armature** — **ARM-012 (small) against the bearing seat, then ARM-011 ×3** above it.
  2. **Set the lower housing (LH-001) onto the armature** so the lower journal (SHF-003) passes down through the lower bearing (BRG-002).
  3. **Invert the assembly and place it in a press.**
  4. **Press the slinger (ARM-010) onto the protruding journal end with slow, INCREMENTAL axial pressure** — ⚠️ **do NOT bottom it out against the housing; leave a small running gap.** Target gap (lower-housing face → slinger) ≈ **0.0255"** (a utility-knife-blade width). Press in small steps and **gauge the gap with a knife blade / feeler** until it just slips in. *(The gap on the as-found machine was not measured before removal — a noted mistake; the 0.0255" value was taken from the restorer's SECOND L&R unit as the reference.)*
- **Tool used (restorer's improvisation):** a **Dillon XL650 progressive reloading press, used as an arbor press** — the press platform/toolhead served as the anvil; a **stainless fender washer (Everbilt, 3/8" ID × 1‑1/4" OD)** was used as the **driver** bearing on the slinger face. The **3/8" (0.375") bore clears the ~0.28" journal**, and the **1‑1/4" OD covers the slinger's ~0.81" face**, so pressure is applied to the slinger, never the shaft tip. *(The fender washer is a TOOL, not a machine part — not in the BOM.)*
- **Accessible-equivalent methods (for the manual — most won't have a reloading press):** the canonical tool is a **1‑ton bench arbor press**; alternatives are a **bench vise closed slowly as a press**, or a **drill-press quill pressed down without rotating.** In every case use a **tubular driver / deep socket / fender washer** whose **bore clears the shaft** and whose **flat face bears on the slinger** — apply slow, even pressure; **never hammer.** The Dillon-press method maps directly to "arbor press + fender-washer driver."
- **Result:** ARM-010 slinger seated to the **~0.0255" gap** (not bottomed); armature mounted in the lower housing — **lower housing COMPLETE.**
- **Follow-on (same session — all TEST-FIT, nothing finalized yet):**
  1. **Upper-housing test-fit:** the armature's upper journal (SHF-002) seats into the upper bearing (BRG-001) — good.
  2. **Brush-port caps/plugs (UH-004):** the two brush-port pieces pushed into the upper-housing brush ports (UH-003) — **finger force, or gentle light taps with a rubber mallet**; both seated. *(Restorer's term: "brush ports"; canonical = UH-004 cap into UH-003 port.)*
  3. **Stator (STR-001) test-fit:** stator set into the upper housing; its **lead clip attaches to one of the brush ports** (field-coil lead → brush connection point — note this for the electrical phase).
  4. **Full motor test-fit:** lower + upper housings joined again — **everything fits and the armature spins FREELY ✓** (resolves the rotor-spins-free check).

### R-10 (cont.) — Reversing behavior confirmed on the bench (2026-06-23)
*As-performed 2026-06-23. Same standalone setup: Rigol DP932A (CH1) low-voltage DC, NOT mains, temporary connections. Resolves the question of WHITE's role and bench-confirms the machine is a reversible model.*
- **✅ FINDING — direction is selected by the RETURN lead, not by supply polarity.** Holding **RED (+) on GREEN** and moving the **(−) lead between BLACK and WHITE** reverses the direction of rotation:
  - **GREEN (+) → BLACK (−):** spins one way (e.g. CW).
  - **GREEN (+) → WHITE (−):** spins the other way (e.g. CCW).
  - (CW/CCW assignment as viewed from a fixed end — record the reference end when the final orientation is fixed; not critical to the wiring logic.)
- **Corrected understanding of the wire map:** **GREEN = the common live lead** (the brush/armature-series end that is always energized). **BLACK and WHITE = the two reversing leads** — the two ends of the field path. Energizing green→black sends current through the field one way; green→white sends it the other way, flipping field polarity relative to the armature → the rotor reverses. This is the classic series/universal-motor reverse-by-swapping-the-field scheme. WHITE is therefore **not** a dead "tap" — it is the second-direction lead. (The earlier R-10 note "WHITE = floating tap" reflected only that white was left open while proving the motor runs in one direction.)
- **Reconciles the earlier "no torque on black–white" result:** black↔white with green open still gives no torque — that path is field-only and leaves the armature/green brush out of circuit. The reversing function keeps **green in circuit at all times** and only switches which of black/white completes the return. Always two leads live, never bridge black+white together.
- **⚠️ Mechanism = best-judgment (no original L&R service manual exists):** most likely the field is brought out as two leads (or a tapped/two-section field) with green as the common armature-side brush lead; the original machine's **red plunger switch** does this black↔white swap as a momentary motor-REVERSE control. Bench behavior is CONFIRMED; the exact internal field topology is (SYN)/inferred until ring-out of the assembled control wiring.
- **Significance:** this is the bench corroboration that **S/N 13505 is a reversible-motor model** — consistent with (a) the 3-wire BLK/WHT/GRN neck loom (forums note the 3-wire loom appears only on reversible units), (b) factory diagram E-1690's green = motor↔rheostat branch, and (c) the operating-manual instruction to *reverse the basket in each jar*. It advances ledger **E5** (plunger = reverse): the reverse FUNCTION is now bench-proven; only the mapping of black/white to the physical plunger/toggle remains for the assembled ring-out.
- **Provenance:** (CONFIRMED) bench test 2026-06-23. Final wiring → R-11 below.
